🌿 Vegetarian & vegan at the reception

The buffet menu is pre-set, but we've pushed for as many veggie-friendly dishes as possible — salads, rice, and plenty of vegetable sides.

One honest heads-up: some Korean dishes use fish-based sauces or anchovy stock, which is hard to avoid in a traditional menu.

If you're vegan, we'll order dedicated vegan food for you — just let us know in advance, in the group chat or by message.

🕊️ The ceremony, step by step (전통혼례 식순)
  1. Procession 신행행렬 — the couple enters in ceremonial attire.
  2. Greeting rituals 친영례 & 전안례 — the groom offers a wooden goose, a symbol of faithfulness.
  3. First performance 1차 축하공연 — traditional music or dance.
  4. Bowing ceremony 교배지례 — deep bows exchanged, for mutual respect.
  5. Respects to parents 서천지례 & 서배우례 — thanking both families.
  6. Sharing of wine 합근지례 — one gourd cup, drunk by both. Now they're one.
  7. Second performance 2차 축하공연 — a blessing for the road ahead.
  8. Exit 신랑·신부 퇴장 — married, and out.
  9. Group photo & feast 기념사진 & 피로연 — your cue to eat.

🎁 About gifts

Many of you are flying across the world to be there. That is the gift — nothing else is expected.

If you'd still like to give something: Korean weddings skip physical presents in favour of 축의금 (chug-ui-geum) — money in an envelope. There's a gift desk in the lobby of the main building where a family member will greet you and look after it.

Typical amounts, for reference:

  • ₩50,000 acquaintances & casual guests
  • ₩100,000 friends & colleagues
  • ₩200,000+ close friends & family

💳 Rather not carry cash? There'll be a PayPal QR code at the gift desk — just scan it and send whatever you'd like. No envelope, no hunting for an ATM.

🚇 Getting around Seoul

T-Money card — buy one at any convenience store (including at the airport). It works on subways, buses, taxis and even shop purchases. Tap in, tap out, done.

The subway is fast, colour-coded, numbered, and announces stops in English. It's genuinely hard to get lost.

Taxis are cheap. ~₩4,800 to start, and a 5 km ride lands around ₩7,400 (~€5). With 3+ people it often beats the subway. Most drivers don't speak English — use Kakao T, or show the address in Korean.

💳 Money & payments

Cards work almost everywhere — restaurants, cafés, shops, transport. Cash is mainly for street-food stalls, markets and small vendors.

Bring roughly €200 per person in cash for a two-week trip and put the rest on your card.

Exchange at the airport — the counters there are run by actual Korean banks, so rates are transparent and locals use them too. Run low later? Any bank branch or ATM will do.

🚄 Trips outside Seoul

KTX high-speed rail reaches Busan, Gyeongju, Daegu and Daejeon from Seoul Station or Yongsan Station. Book up to a month ahead on KORAIL ↗.

Intercity buses reach the smaller towns KTX misses — book on KOBUS ↗. Terminals: Seoul Express (south), Dong Seoul (east), Nambu (southwest).

Jeju Island — flights leave Gimpo and Incheon roughly every 15 minutes and take about an hour. Korean Air, Asiana, Jeju Air, T'way.

Booking sometimes fails without a Korean ID or phone number. If that happens, just ask us — we'll book it for you. Carry your passport for ticket pickup.

🎎 Etiquette, SIM cards & emergencies

Good to know

  • A slight bow is the standard greeting.
  • Shoes off in homes and many traditional restaurants.
  • No tipping — ever. It's not expected anywhere.
  • Public bins are rare; carry a small bag for rubbish.
  • Keep quiet on the subway, and leave the priority seats free.

Staying connected

Grab a SIM or rent pocket Wi-Fi at Incheon Airport. Free Wi-Fi is everywhere — cafés, subways, public spaces.
